# Cover Letter Question Types

Classify every Korean cover letter question before drafting. Use the question
type to choose the answer frame, not to force keywords into the answer.

| Type | Common Question Signal | Evaluation Intent | Answer Frame | Resume Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Experience | experience, example, challenge, failure, achievement | concrete behavior and repeatable action | STAR or CAR: situation, task/problem, action, result | Work Experience or Projects entries, strength comments, metrics |
| Strength | strength, capability, advantage, differentiation | capability with proof | strength, evidence, role application | repeated behavior, feedback, result |
| Motivation | why this company, why this role, interest trigger | company/role understanding and fit | company/role understanding, personal connection, contribution plan | Target Role, Summary, related Work Experience or Projects |
| Role Fit | role, duty, contribution, execution | job capability and role understanding | role understanding, evidence mapping, growth or contribution plan | role-relevant work, projects, tools, metrics |
| Collaboration | collaboration, conflict, persuasion, coordination | working with others under constraints | shared goal, conflict/context, action, result, learning | collaboration, conflict, stakeholder work |
| Problem Solving | problem, improvement, solution, cause | problem definition and execution quality | problem, diagnosis, action, result, prevention | problem, action, result, prevention |
| Values | values, principle, responsibility, ethics | judgment and trust | principle, difficult choice, action, consequence | principle-based decisions, responsibility examples |
| Future Plan | after joining, plan, growth, vision | realistic future contribution | near-term contribution, growth path, evidence basis | Target Role, Summary, strongest role-relevant evidence |
| Job Change | 이직 사유, reason for leaving, career transition | career direction and retention risk | work the candidate wants to do, what this posting offers, consistency with history | Work Experience progression, Target Role |

`Job Change` applies to experienced candidates only. See `Reason For Changing
Jobs` in `career-type-playbooks.md` before drafting it.

## Failure And Mistake Answers

Korean cover letters and interviews regularly ask for 실패 경험, 아쉬웠던 경험,
성장과정, or 보완하고 싶은 점. Screeners use these to test ownership and
judgment, not to collect confessions. The common failure modes are opposite
extremes: hiding the mistake behind a passive summary, or over-confessing a
problem the candidate cannot show they contained.

Write these answers with:

- **A decision the candidate owned.** Name the choice they made, not the
  circumstance that happened to them. `일정이 촉박했습니다` is context;
  `검증 범위를 줄이기로 판단했습니다` is a decision.
- **Honest cause.** State why the decision was wrong or incomplete. Do not
  attribute the cause to a teammate, manager, professor, or client. Shifting
  blame reads worse than the mistake.
- **What changed afterward.** A concrete behavior, check, process, or habit the
  candidate adopted. This is the part screeners actually evaluate.
- **Evidence that the change stuck.** A later situation where the new behavior
  applied. Without this, the answer reads as a rehearsed lesson.

Keep out of these answers:

- Failures that imply a trust, safety, ethics, or confidentiality problem.
- Failures the candidate never resolved or cannot describe the end state of.
- Fake-weakness framing such as `너무 완벽주의라서` or `일에 너무 몰입해서`.
- Any detail not supported by `resume.md` or user-provided context. If the
  learning is real but the situation is missing, ask for it instead of
  reconstructing a plausible story.

Answer frame: decision, honest cause, what changed, where the change showed up
later.

## Sub-Role Awareness

Korean postings are often narrower than the job family implies: 백엔드 중 결제
정산, 반도체 공정기술, 마케팅 중 퍼포먼스, 영업 중 기술영업. Screeners read a
strong answer aimed at the wrong sub-role as low 직무 이해, which damages the
application even when the evidence is genuinely good.

Before drafting Role Fit, Motivation, or Future Plan answers:

- Identify the narrowest role scope the posting actually names, not the job
  family in the posting title.
- Lead with the `resume.md` evidence closest to that scope, even when a
  different experience is the candidate's strongest overall.
- When the strongest evidence sits in an adjacent sub-domain, connect it
  explicitly rather than leaving the reader to infer the link. Name the
  transferable mechanism, not a generic claim of adaptability.
- If `resume.md` has no evidence near the named scope, say so to the user
  before drafting. Do not stretch an unrelated experience to fit.

## Length Handling

- Count Korean characters with spaces unless the company or question specifies
  another rule.
- If a limit exists, target 90-98% of the limit.
- If the draft exceeds the limit, compress the lowest-impact sentence first.
- If the draft is too short, add only resume-backed specifics.

## Natural Korean Writing

- Prefer concrete verbs over broad attitudes.
- Avoid generic passion or growth claims unless paired with resume-backed
  behavior, method, or result.
- Keep each sentence interview-defensible from `resume.md`.
