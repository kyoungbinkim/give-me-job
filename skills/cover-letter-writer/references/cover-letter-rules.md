# Cover Letter Rules

## Writing Principles

- Start from what the employer needs, then connect the candidate's evidence.
- Use concrete problem, action, and result.
- Prefer one strong experience over several shallow examples.
- Keep the company fit natural. Do not insert values keywords mechanically.
- Use metrics when present. If metrics are absent, ask about scale, frequency, period, users, throughput, or comparison before inventing numbers.
- Make each paragraph defensible in an interview.
- Use 3C4P as an analysis tool when the experience needs sharper logic, but do not expose all 3C4P labels in the final cover letter unless the user asks.

## Safety Checks

- Does every strong claim map to a `resume.md` evidence ID?
- Is there any company name from another application?
- Is there any sub-role, 직무, team, or domain name from another application?
- Does the answer target the narrowest sub-role the posting names, rather than the job family?
- Is the candidate claiming a skill that appears nowhere in `resume.md`?
- Is the answer responding to the actual question?
- If the question asks how the candidate decided, does the answer show the judgment rather than only the outcome?
- If the answer describes a failure, does it own a decision, avoid blaming others, and end on a concrete change?
- Is the sentence readable for an HR reviewer?
- Is the conclusion about company contribution specific enough?
- If 3C4P was used, are Customer, Company, Competitor, and 4P elements based on real evidence rather than retrofitted claims?

## Source Guidance

The writing strategy follows these summarized sources:

- STAFFS STORY Naver blog, 2024-07-30: emphasize company research, JD keywords, STAR structure, measurable results, differentiation, logical flow, and matching company needs to applicant experience.
- Public-employment cover letter guidance: treat the cover letter as interview source material that shows role fit, company fit, motivation, and future contribution.
- 3C4P cover letter interpretation from the user's notes: use Customer, Company, Competitor, Product, Place, Price, and Promotion to break down an experience into a persuasive 필살기 with measurable proof.
- Screener-perspective notes from the user: reward owned mistakes and visible reasoning, and treat answers aimed at the wrong sub-role as weak 직무 이해. Applied to failure answers, sub-role targeting, and decision-quality checks only. Resume-scanning advice from the same notes was not adopted, because `resume.md` here is an internal evidence source rather than a document submitted to a screener.
- Experienced-hire screener notes from the user: weight organizational fit alongside technical depth, expect achievements rather than duties, and treat an unexplained job-change pattern or previous-employer criticism as a rejection signal. Applied to the experienced playbook and the HR-review criteria. Survey percentages and preferred-years-of-experience ranges from the same notes were not adopted: they are unsourced, and this project neither states unverifiable numbers as fact nor advises a candidate on how desirable their own tenure is.

Do not quote either source in generated cover letters.
